      How the numbers are calculated
      • Gross sales = baseline gross × (1 + price change) × (1 + volume change)
      • Net revenue = gross × (1 − baseline cancellation rate)
      • Cost = cost share × baseline net revenue × (1 + volume change); unit cost unchanged
      • Profit = net revenue − cost
      • Break-even volume = (1 − cost share) / (1 + price change − cost share) − 1

        How segments are defined
        • Recency = days from the customer's last order to the day after the last invoice in the data. Frequency = number of orders. Monetary = gross product sales.
        • Each is scored 1 to 5 (5 = best) from the share of customers at or below that value, so customers with the same value always get the same score.
        • Champions: recency and frequency scores both 4-5. Loyal: both 3 or more. Recent: recency 4-5, low frequency. At risk: recency 1-2, frequency 3 or more. Inactive: recency 1-2, low frequency. Needs attention: everyone else.
        • These are descriptive labels for the past. They do not predict who will buy again.

        What this page cannot tell you

        • Everyone. Only orders with a customer ID are used; the rest (guest checkouts) cannot be followed over time.
        • True new customers in Dec 2009. The data starts in Dec 2009, so that group includes people who had already been buying before. It is marked with *.
        • Churn. "Inactive" only means no order up to the end of the data. Some of these customers may order later.
        • Net value. Customer values are gross sales; credit notes are not matched to customers here.
        • Who the customers are. Many large accounts are probably businesses buying wholesale, so a few customers can be very large.
